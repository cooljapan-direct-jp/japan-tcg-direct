How to Buy ONE PIECE Cards from Japan

The ONE PIECE Card Game launched first in Japan, and many collectors abroad look for Japanese-edition and parallel cards. This page covers Japanese keywords for searching on Buyee and what to check in a listing.

Why search in Japan

Japanese-edition cards
Japanese-edition booster packs and cards handed out at events and tournaments in Japan include many that are scarce abroad.
Parallels and high rarities
Popular cards such as parallels (alternate art) and secret rares are listed in large numbers as singles.
Boxes and bulk lots
You can also find listings for buying in bulk, such as sealed boxes and whole decks.

Japanese search keywords for Buyee

Buyee can be searched in English, but searching with Japanese keywords also finds listings that sellers wrote only in Japanese.

KeywordMeaning / how to use
ワンピースカード ／ ワンピカードCommon names for the ONE PIECE Card Game. Search both to miss fewer listings.
パラレルCards with different art or finish from the regular version.
コミパラNickname for parallel cards using panels from the original manga. Very popular and often expensive.
SECSecret rare, a rarity label.
リーダーLeader cards, the center of a deck.
未開封BOX ／ カートンA sealed box, or a case containing several boxes.
美品“Good condition”, but it is the seller's own judgment.
PSA10 ／ 鑑定品Cards graded by a grading company. Always check the certification number.
まとめ売りSeveral cards sold together as a lot.

Checkpoints when viewing a listing

  • Use the card number and rarity label to check whether it is a regular or parallel version
  • Check the photos for scratches and whitening on the front, back, corners and edges
  • Check whether it is the Japanese or English edition (prices can differ for the same card name)
  • “Graded” is the seller's own claim. Verify the certification number on the grading company's website
  • Be careful with listings far below market price or with few photos
  • Before bidding, estimate the total including Buyee fees, international shipping and import duties
