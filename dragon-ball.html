How to Buy Dragon Ball Cards from Japan

Several Dragon Ball card series have been released in Japan over many years. Gameplay and prices differ by series, so it helps to know which series a card belongs to before you search.

Main series

Fusion World
The current series of the Dragon Ball Super Card Game, released as booster packs and starter decks.
Dragon Ball Heroes series
Cards for a Japanese arcade game. Because they are released mainly in Japan, many are hard to get abroad.
Older series
Collectible cards released in the past, such as Carddass. For vintage cards, condition makes a big difference in value.

Japanese search keywords for Buyee

Buyee can be searched in English, but searching with Japanese keywords also finds listings that sellers wrote only in Japanese.

KeywordMeaning / how to use
ドラゴンボールカードThe basic keyword for searching widely across all series.
フュージョンワールドName of the current Dragon Ball Super Card Game series.
ドラゴンボールヒーローズ ／ SDBHSeries name and abbreviation for the arcade game cards.
カードダスAn older collectible series. Use it when looking for vintage cards.
UR ／ SEC ／ パラレルRarity and alternate-art labels. Combine them with the card name.
未開封Unopened. Use it when looking for boxes or packs.
美品“Good condition”, but it is the seller's own judgment.
PSA10 ／ 鑑定品Cards graded by a grading company. Always check the certification number.
まとめ売りSeveral cards sold together as a lot.

Checkpoints when viewing a listing

  • Check which series the card belongs to (prices differ by series even for the same name)
  • Check the photos for scratches and whitening on the front, back, corners and edges
  • “Graded” is the seller's own claim. Verify the certification number on the grading company's website
  • For vintage cards, watch for sun fading and warping that are hard to see in photos
  • Be careful with listings far below market price or with few photos
  • Before bidding, estimate the total including Buyee fees, international shipping and import duties
